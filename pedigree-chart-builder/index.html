💡 Why Consanguinity Increases Autosomal Recessive Risk

In standard pedigrees, a double horizontal line denotes consanguinity (mating between blood relatives, such as first or second cousins). Because related partners inherit segments of their genome identical-by-descent from a common ancestor, the probability that both partners carry the identical rare recessive mutation (like CFTR ΔF508) is substantially higher than in the general population.

Hallmarks of Autosomal Dominant Inheritance

  • Vertical Transmission: Affected individuals are present in consecutive generations (I → II → III). Does not typically skip generations.
  • Equal Sex Distribution: Males and females have an equal 50% probability of receiving the mutant allele from an affected parent.
  • Absence of Unaffected Carriers: Anyone who inherits an expanded allele (≥40 repeats) will eventually develop the clinical phenotype if they live to the age of onset. Heterozygotes are affected.
  • Anticipation: Paternal transmission of the mutant allele can lead to further trinucleotide repeat expansion, causing earlier age of onset in subsequent generations.

Standard Clinical Pedigree Nomenclature

International conventions for mapping genetic history (Recommendations of the National Society of Genetic Counselors)

Symbol Standard Clinical Convention Clinical Meaning & Usage Notes
Square: Male Represents a biological male. Displayed as an open square when unaffected.
Circle: Female Represents a biological female. Displayed as an open circle when unaffected.
Diamond: Unspecified sex Used when biological sex is unknown, unrecorded, or during early pregnancy counseling before fetal sex is determined.
Unshaded: Unaffected individual Denotes an individual who does not manifest the clinical phenotype under study.
Shaded (Magenta): Affected individual Denotes an individual displaying the clinical phenotype of the disorder (e.g., active Cystic Fibrosis or manifest Huntington's Chorea). In standard medical genetics, a clear legend must define the color.
Half-shaded / Centered Dot: Carrier Denotes an unaffected heterozygous carrier of an autosomal recessive gene (e.g., CFTR wt/ΔF508) or X-linked carrier female (centered dot).
Slashed Symbol: Deceased individual A diagonal slash from bottom-left to top-right indicates the individual is deceased. Often annotated with age at death (e.g., "d. 57y").
Single Horizontal Line: Mating Connects two individuals representing a reproductive partnership / biological union.
Double Horizontal Line: Consanguinity Mating between blood relatives (e.g. first cousins). Significantly increases the probability of homozygous rare recessive conditions.
Arrow: Proband (Consultand) Points to the index case or consultand who first brought the family to medical or genetic attention. Annotated with "P".
I II III
Roman Numerals (I, II, III): Generational rows Identifies generational tiers placed along the left margin. Individuals within each generation are numbered sequentially from left to right (e.g., II-3).
